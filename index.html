Password Security Tips

Best practices to keep your accounts secure

Never Reuse Passwords

Use unique passwords for each account to prevent credential stuffing attacks.

Enable Multi-Factor Authentication

Add an extra layer of security with 2FA/MFA on all your important accounts.

Use Password Managers

Store passwords securely in encrypted vaults instead of writing them down.

Avoid Personal Information

Don't use birthdays, names, or personal details that can be easily guessed.

Update Passwords Regularly

Change critical passwords every 3-6 months or after any security breach.

Create Unique Passwords

Use long, complex passwords with a mix of characters for maximum security.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about password security

A strong password is at least 12 characters long, includes a mix of uppercase and lowercase letters, numbers, and special characters. It should avoid dictionary words, personal information, and common patterns. Strong passwords have high entropy and are difficult to guess or crack.

Dictionary words are vulnerable to dictionary attacks, where hackers use lists of common words and phrases to guess passwords. Even with substitutions (like replacing 'a' with '@'), these passwords can be cracked quickly. Random character combinations are much more secure.

Special characters significantly increase the complexity of passwords. They expand the character set from just 26 letters to over 90 possible characters, dramatically increasing the number of possible combinations and making brute force attacks much more difficult and time-consuming.

Modern security guidelines suggest changing passwords only when there's a suspected breach or compromise. For critical accounts, consider updating every 3-6 months. Focus on creating strong, unique passwords rather than frequent changes, which can lead to weaker password choices.

Using a password manager helps you securely store and organize your passwords in one encrypted vault. It allows you to create long, unique passwords for every account without needing to remember them all. Most password managers also offer features such as password generation, autofill, breach alerts, and secure synchronization across devices. This greatly improves both convenience and online security.
