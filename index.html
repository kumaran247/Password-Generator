Cybersecurity Standards

Password Defense Best Practices

Follow these essential cybersecurity hygiene habits recommended by global intelligence agencies and security engineers.

Eliminate Credential Reuse

Reusing passwords across services makes you vulnerable to credential stuffing when one third-party service suffers a data leak.

Enforce Hardware 2FA / FIDO

Pair strong passwords with hardware security keys (YubiKey) or TOTP authenticator apps instead of SMS verification.

Adopt Encrypted Vaults

Store your high-entropy credentials in zero-knowledge password managers (e.g. 1Password, Bitwarden, KeePassXC).

Zero Personal Tokens

Never base passwords or PINs on birth years, pet names, street addresses, or easily discoverable OSINT information.

Length Trumps Complexity

A 20-character passphrase with spaces or hyphens yields vastly greater entropy than a short, complex 8-character string.

Check Data Breaches

Periodically audit your primary email accounts on HaveIBeenPwned and rotate passwords immediately if a breach occurs.

Password entropy measures the mathematical unpredictability and information density of a password in bits. A password with 60+ bits of entropy is resistant to standard attacks, while 80+ bits is considered enterprise-grade, and 100+ bits is virtually uncrackable by modern supercomputers for billions of years.

Passphrases combine multiple random real words (like correct-horse-battery-staple). Because humans can easily remember words while computers still have to search through millions of combinatorial word permutations, passphrases offer exceptionally high entropy while being remarkably effortless to type on mobile devices.

Crack times are computed based on the total possible combinations (\(2^{\text{entropy}}\)) divided by real-world cracking speeds: from rate-limited online web services (100 guesses/sec) to high-speed multi-GPU clusters (10 billion guesses/sec) and distributed supercomputing clusters (100 trillion guesses/sec).

Typing a 24-character random password onto a smartphone keyboard is slow and prone to errors. Our Offline QR code is generated purely on an HTML5 canvas without using any external Google or third-party tracking APIs. You can point your phone camera at your screen to instantly scan the password into your mobile vault securely.

Modern cracking tools like Hashcat and John the Ripper use rule-based hybrid attacks that test keyboard walks, numerical increments, and dictionary leet-speak substitutions first. SecurePass checks and penalizes these patterns to protect you against targeted heuristic attacks.
